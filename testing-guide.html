5-Test Battery · No Equipment Beyond a Tape Measure & Stopwatch

Testing Guide

No 1RM needed anywhere in this program — these 5 tests are what tell you the numbers are working. Run the full battery before you start, then retest to confirm progress.

When to test

Full battery: pre-season week 1, mid-season (if season is 20+ weeks), post-season. Abbreviated battery (10m sprint + CMJ only): every 4-6 weeks through the off-season.

Don't test: within 48h of a match, the day of/after a high-volume training session, when sick/injured/sleep-disrupted, or before a deload week — test after the deload, when you're freshest, not before it.

Compare: athlete to their own prior score first, then position norms, then the general tables below. One test is a data point, not a profile — flag any left/right asymmetry over 10-15% as a priority, regardless of the absolute number.

Test order (one session, ~45min)

Full dynamic warm-up first (see exercise library) → CMJ (3 trials) → Broad Jump (3 trials) → SL Broad Jump (3/leg) → SL Lateral Bound (3/leg) → 10m Sprint (3 trials, last — most CNS-demanding). Rest 3-5min between test types; short rest wrecks reliability more than people think.

1. 10m Sprint

Evidence: ⭐⭐ Moderate — standard sprint testing protocol, well-established norms.

Protocol: Timing gates (or stopwatch + tape). 2-point staggered start, front foot 0.3m behind the start line — no rolling start. 3 trials, 3-5min rest between, record best time to 0.01s.

Why it matters for a GK: Reaction and first-step quickness matter more than top-end speed here — GK 10m times sit slower than every other position on the general scale, and that's normal, not a red flag.

2. Countermovement Jump (CMJ)

Evidence: ⭐⭐ Moderate — reliable field test, jump mat or phone flight-time app (e.g. My Jump 2) both valid.

Protocol: Hands on hips, no arm swing (keep it consistent every test — arm swing adds 5-10cm and invalidates comparison). 3 trials, 30-60s rest, record best.

Why it matters for a GK: Highest jump demand of any position on the pitch — aerial duels and diving both load through this quality. GK elite average sits 42-50cm.

3. Broad Jump (Standing Long Jump)

Evidence: ⭐⭐ Moderate — reliable field test of horizontal power.

Protocol: Stand behind the line, two-foot takeoff, arm swing allowed. Measure from the line to the heel closest to the start on landing. 3 attempts, record best.

4. Single-Leg Broad Jump

Evidence: ⭐ Coach-practical — no established population norms; use as an asymmetry/durability tracker, not an absolute standard.

Protocol: Single-leg takeoff, single-leg landing, must stick (no hop or step). 3 attempts per leg, rest between legs, record best per leg.

How to read it: Rough reference point — a single-leg distance around 65-75% of your bilateral broad jump is typical. The number that actually matters is left vs. right: flag anything over a 10-15% gap between legs as a priority-fix, especially post-injury.

5. Single-Leg Lateral Bound

Evidence: ⭐ Coach-practical — no established population norms; asymmetry/durability tracker specific to the cutting and push-off pattern behind a dive.

Protocol: Single-leg lateral takeoff, single-leg landing, must stick. Measure lateral distance. 3 attempts per leg, record best per leg.

Why it's in a GK battery specifically: This is the closest field test to a dive push-off. Track it over time per leg — a widening L/R gap here often shows up before it shows up anywhere else.

Reading the results

A test rated "Below Average" or "Poor" isn't a verdict — it's a training priority. Feed it back into your tier/emphasis choice on the off-season and in-season pages. A 10-15%+ left/right gap on any test matters more than where you sit on the population table.
